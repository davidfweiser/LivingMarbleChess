What's in the game

Every capture is a fight

Pieces hop, lean and turn to face each other. Each kind knows three attacks — a bull charge, a joust, a judgment beam, a cannon shot, a frost nova, a rain of golden swords — and the loser breaks into real fragments that tumble across the board.

Five armies

Carved Staunton marble; a medieval army of men-at-arms, knights on horseback, bishops on their thrones and a gatehouse with a dragon inside; the aliens: little green men, flying saucers and big-brained psychics floating in glass jars; the Romans: legionaries in cloaks with spears and swords, war chariots, a wooden siege tower, the empress and the emperor — who fight hand to hand; and the dinosaurs: feathered raptors, pteranodons, triceratops, a brachiosaurus, the spinosaurus queen and the tyrannosaurus king — who fight tooth and claw.

The fallen come back

A captured piece returns as a translucent ghost beside the board, watches the rest of the game and cheers its side on.

Music for every piece

Three themes for every piece in every army — concert-hall classical, early music from the castle, 1950s science fiction with a theremin, sword-and-sandal epics and a lost world of horns and great drums — and when a piece moves, its music takes over.

Play your way

Against the computer at five strengths, a friend on the same screen, a friend online with a link — or anyone online: pick a picture, meet the other players in the lobby, look at their stats and ask them to play, in rated games with clocks, a rating and a leaderboard.

Easy to play

Click a piece, then a glowing square. Drag to turn the board, even to look underneath; scroll to zoom. Press T for a hint and F1 for help. On an iPhone or iPad, tap, drag and pinch. Runs in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari on a computer, and in Safari on iPhone and iPad.
